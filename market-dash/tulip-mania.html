1

When a setup can fire (all of these)

  • Tuesday to Friday, 7:35–8:35 am PT.
  • Price tests a sticky level: a floor buys a call, a ceiling buys a put. Levels are GEX walls, max ±gamma, gamma flip, OI magnet and yesterday's high, low and close; a level is sticky once it has sat there 15 minutes.
  • The level is armed: price was at least 0.4U away from it before this touch.
  • Price is near VWAP at the touch: −0.37U to +0.05U on the side of the trade (U = the expected 30-minute move).
2

The three checks

  • Wall clear. The level is not just yesterday's high, low or close with a GEX wall 15–50¢ past it. Price tends to run to that wall first, right where the stop sits. (Your find.)
  • Volume drying up. Volume over the last 5 minutes is under 1.0× the 30 minutes before. Rising volume into the level means the push is real.
  • 1-minute turn. The 1-min SMA 4 is above SMA 7 for a call (below for a put) at the touch. The bounce has already started. (Your MAs.)

The trade

  • 0DTE, 1 strike out of the money (about $0.70–$1.20, how you buy; the backtest averaged $1.13). Don't go cheaper than 1 OTM.
  • Stop −$0.30 · target +$2.00 · out within 60 minutes.
  • At most 2 trades a day, the next only after the last one closed. One loss = done for the day.
  • No setup = no trade. Sitting out costs $0.

Is it working by 8 minutes?

Every home run was up +$0.20 within 3–8 minutes (Aug 18: 3, Aug 21: 5, Sep 9: 5, Sep 29: 8). Losers sat 5–18 minutes before the stop and only one (Aug 19) ever reached +$0.20.

Possible rule: not up +$0.20 by 8 minutes → tighten to −$0.15 or get out. Only if it never hurts a winner. Breakeven at +$0.20 would have saved Aug 19.

30-minute exhaustion

When the last 30-min candle (or the 30-min 2/10 EMAs) already pointed our way, level bounces averaged −$24; when the 30-min had pushed into the level, +$3. Strong as a "stay away" read, but inside the home-run setups it would cut Sep 9, so it isn't a rule.
